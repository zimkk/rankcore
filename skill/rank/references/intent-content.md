# Intent and Content Architecture

Verified: 2026-09-21  
Review-after: 2027-03-21  
Authority: official-docs + internal synthesis

## Start from the product

Before proposing content, understand:
- what the product/service actually does;
- who uses it;
- who buys it;
- why it is chosen;
- what alternatives exist;
- geography/language;
- proof available in the repository/business;
- conversion actions.

Do not create a content strategy detached from the product.

## Demand model

Build opportunity clusters around user jobs:

- category/problem education;
- solution evaluation;
- product/category comparisons;
- named alternatives;
- use cases;
- roles/industries;
- integrations;
- migration;
- implementation/setup;
- support/troubleshooting;
- pricing/cost/value;
- templates/examples/tools;
- original data/research;
- local or international needs where real.

## Query research without fake precision

When the host agent can browse, inspect representative search results and current language.

Without a connected keyword-data provider:
- use qualitative demand evidence;
- do not invent volume, CPC, difficulty, traffic potential, or rank positions.

If a provider is connected, label:
- provider;
- metric name;
- date;
- geography;
- estimation caveats.

## Page-to-intent mapping

Prefer one strong page for one coherent job rather than many slight variants.

Good candidates often include:
- core product/category page;
- feature pages where distinct user value exists;
- use-case pages;
- integration pages with real integration details;
- comparison/alternative pages that are fair and substantive;
- support/documentation;
- original research/data;
- tools/calculators/templates;
- local pages only where a real local offering exists.

## Comparison and alternative pages

Allowed and useful when:
- genuinely helping evaluation;
- accurately representing competitors;
- distinguishing facts from claims;
- showing dated sources where claims can change;
- including meaningful criteria and tradeoffs;
- avoiding deceptive trademarks/impersonation.

Do not create dozens of near-identical “X vs Y” shells with commodity copy.

## Programmatic content value gate

Before generating many pages, require multiple sources of differentiated value such as:
- unique structured data;
- real local inventory;
- real integration-specific steps;
- proprietary analysis;
- user-generated data with moderation;
- calculations/tools;
- meaningful product-specific output.

Reject scale where pages mostly substitute:
- city names;
- keyword phrases;
- industry labels;
- product names;
without materially changing usefulness.

## Original and non-commodity value

Generative search makes commodity summaries less defensible.

Prefer:
- first-party expertise;
- actual implementation details;
- experiments;
- benchmarks;
- customer evidence with permission;
- methods;
- original visuals;
- datasets;
- examples;
- tools;
- strong point of view grounded in evidence.

Never fabricate:
- customers;
- quotes;
- awards;
- benchmarks;
- authors;
- credentials;
- data.

## Information architecture

Use topic/page relationships to help users navigate, not to manufacture a “topical authority score.”

Ensure related pages connect naturally:
- product ↔ use case;
- product ↔ integration;
- guide ↔ relevant product capability;
- comparison ↔ source evidence;
- docs ↔ feature/product pages where appropriate.

## Refresh strategy

Refresh when:
- facts change;
- screenshots/steps become stale;
- competitive claims change;
- product behavior changes;
- search intent shifts;
- regulation/pricing/specifications change.

Do not update dates without substantive review.

## AEO/GEO perspective

Treat AEO/GEO as presentation and evidence disciplines layered on solid search/content fundamentals:
- direct answers where useful;
- clear headings;
- concise definitions;
- explicit entities/relationships;
- visible evidence;
- quotable factual statements;
- original value;
- source transparency.

Do not create awkward “AI chunks” solely to game answer engines.
